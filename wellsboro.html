A new volume in the Life in Wellsboro series

Life in Wellsboro 1960–2000

Gale Largey, with George Loughery, is preparing a third pictorial history of Wellsboro, Pennsylvania — a continuation of Life in Wellsboro, 1880–1920 and Life in Wellsboro, 1920–1960.

About the book

This new volume will tell the story of Wellsboro from 1960 through 2000 through photographs, names, captions, and community memory. Like the earlier books, it will be a visual history first: a book meant to be opened at the kitchen table, passed from neighbor to neighbor, and used to remember the people who made Wellsboro what it was.

The book will focus less on buildings alone and more on people in action — working, teaching, farming, serving, marching, coaching, volunteering, celebrating, responding to emergencies, and living everyday life.

We are especially interested in photographs that make someone say, “I remember milk delivery!,” “She was my Mom's teacher and me too!,” “My father worked there,” or “We were standing right across the street.”

What we hope to include

The Place

Main Street, the courthouse square, schools, churches, neighborhoods, farms, parks, winter streets, and the changing look of Wellsboro.

The People

Teachers, nurses, firefighters, police officers, veterans, farmers, coaches, students, clergy, volunteers, borough workers, and families.

Everyday Life

School days, Little League, church suppers, hunting camp, snowstorms, paper routes, parades, dances, family gatherings, and workdays.

Main Events

The Laurel Festival, Vietnam era, moon landing, Hurricane Agnes, Bicentennial, STPR rally, Dickens of a Christmas, and the turn of the millennium.

Share photographs and memories

Do you have photographs of Wellsboro life between 1960 and 2000?

We are looking for family photographs, school photographs, workplace photographs, festival photographs, church photographs, parade photographs, sports photographs, fire company photographs, snowstorm photographs, hunting camp photographs, neighborhood photographs, and pictures of people doing the everyday work of the town. Photographs may be color or black and white; however, all photographs will be published in black and white.

Please include, if known:

  • Names of people in the photograph
  • Year or approximate year
  • Location
  • Event or activity shown
  • Photographer or owner of the photograph
  • Permission to consider the photograph for publication
